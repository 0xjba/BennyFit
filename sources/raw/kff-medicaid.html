Status of State Medicaid Expansion Decisions

Published: Aug 21, 2026
Email Add KFF on Google

The Affordable Care Act’s (ACA) Medicaid expansion expanded Medicaid coverage to nearly all adults with incomes up to 138% of the Federal Poverty Level ($21,597 for an individual in 2025) and provided states with an enhanced federal matching rate (FMAP) for their expansion populations.

To date, 41 states (including DC)   have adopted   the Medicaid expansion and 10 states   have not adopted   the expansion. Current status for each state is based on KFF tracking and analysis of state expansion activity.

These data are also available in a table format. The map may be downloaded as a Powerpoint.

Status of State Action on the Medicaid Expansion Decision

* State has a trigger law that would end expansion coverage or require states to take steps to mitigate increases in state costs if federal funding for the expansion is reduced.

Key States with Expansion Activity

This table tracks current state actions related to adoption and implementation ofthe Affordable Care Act (ACA) Medicaid expansion. Information about original adoption and implementation of the ACA expansion can be found on State Health Facts. For non-expansion states, we include recent activity related to debate about adopting the ACA expansion. Actions captured in the tracker include legislative actions (passage of bills in at least one legislative chamber or committee), executive actions (Governor’s budget proposal, executive orders, or other significant announcements), and voter-led actions (progress toward ballot initiatives). We also mention some limited coverage expansions that would not qualify as full ACA Medicaid expansions and would not qualify for enhanced federal matching funds. For expansion states, we include recent actions that would place limits or new requirements on the expansion population, especially through Section 1115 waivers.

^State has recent activity related to “trigger” laws tied to the share of federal funding. South Dakota and West Virginia are or were considering action to put a trigger law in place. In Virginia, a budget amendment to remove automatic termination of the expansion from the state’s trigger law and instead require a legislative committee to develop recommendations on ways to adjust to the loss of federal funding was introduced but not included in the final budget. Proposals to remove or scale back trigger laws have been introduced in Illinois and North Carolina.
